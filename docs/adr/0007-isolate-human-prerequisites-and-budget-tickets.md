# Isolate human prerequisites in one ticket and give every ticket a minute budget

Account access, database provisioning, and model credentials are the only parts of the plan that require a person, and they were previously folded into the first implementation ticket, which made every other ticket wait on a human from the first minute. They now live in a dedicated provisioning ticket ([issue 000](../../issues/000-provision-hosting-and-model-access.md)). Application scaffolding, isolated session state, seeded knowledge, and reset proceed locally without it; only the hosted-deployment criterion of the foundation ticket depends on it. The hosted-verification ticket remains the second and last point of required human judgment.

The three-hour budget covers implementation, deployment, verification, and submission materials. Divided across the required tickets it leaves roughly fifteen to thirty minutes each, so every ticket now records its share of the 180 minutes and a cut line stating what to drop first if that share is exceeded. The budgets are estimates that will be wrong in detail; their purpose is to stop early tickets from consuming the time the central improvement loop needs. Matching counts remain required but are scheduled after the loop, and decision time stays the first feature cut, as the [prototype plan](../prototype-plan.md) already records.

Ticket boundaries are otherwise unchanged. Answer generation and usage accounting remain one ticket because usage enforcement is the gate before public model access and a single sequential implementer gains nothing from splitting them; the split is noted as the first change to make if implementation runs in parallel.
